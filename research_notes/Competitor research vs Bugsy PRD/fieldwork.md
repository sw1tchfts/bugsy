## 1. Company: owner, founding, funding, headquarters, size, trades served

### Takeaway
Fieldwork is a small, privately held, family-run product of Fieldwork Software, LLC in Niles, Illinois. It is tied to Anstar Products, Inc., a pest control products maker at the same street address. No outside funding or acquisition was found. ## 2. Wedge features: status and source for each, and exactly which state reports it produces

That makes CMP-02 a full "Yes". It also produces two state-format reports: California's Monthly Summary Use Report and New York's annual NYSDEC pesticide report (CSV). It has no general ledger (one-way QuickBooks Online sync), no documented deferred revenue, proration, restricted-use controls or license expiry tracking. Skill-based assignment exists only on the Enterprise plan.

- The SCH-04 distinction matters for the pitch. Fieldwork matches on *skills*, on its most expensive plan, with no license number or expiry. Bugsy's message should be "license category and expiry enforced at dispatch, on every plan".

## 4. Pricing: tiers, billable user, inclusions and add-ons, processing, contracts, fees

### Takeaway
The public pricing page shows $59, $79 and $99 a month "for 1 field user" with free office users. Fieldwork's own help center (May 21, 2026) shows the actual structure: five plans, each a **base price plus a smaller per-additional-technician price**. They are Solo $39+$19, Startup $59+$29, Business $79+$39, Pro $99+$49 and Enterprise $149+$79. The earlier notes' "$59 to $99 per field user" reading overstates the cost: an 8-technician company pays $352 on Business, $442 on Pro or $702 on Enterprise, against Bugsy's $632 to $792. Texting, AI and data import beyond the customer list cost extra. Contracts are month to month, with no sign-up or training fee.

### Cited Findings
**Published tiers**
- Public pricing page:
  - Startup $59 a month, Business $79 ("Best Value") and Pro $99, under a heading of "I have 1 field user(s)". The page's text does not show how the price scales with more users.
  - Startup includes free office users, phone and chat support, CRM, calendar, invoicing, mobile apps, customer portal, reporting, work order photos, reminders, QuickBooks integration and custom forms.
  - Business adds Google mapping, routing optimization and device management.

### Inferences
- **Threat to Bugsy's first-customer segment: high on price and channel, medium on features.** Fieldwork is cheaper at every size in the 3 to 15 technician band except on its Enterprise tier. It already has Wisconsin customers and trade-show presence, and its support reputation is excellent. Its gaps are Bugsy's whole wedge: Wisconsin ATCP 29 records, notices, license enforcement, restricted-use controls, a general ledger and deferred revenue. A Wisconsin owner who feels compliance and accounting pain is still Bugsy's buyer. An owner who mainly wants cheap, solid scheduling and billing will likely choose Fieldwork.
- The most effective response is probably to position Bugsy against Fieldwork Enterprise ($702 for 8 techs) rather than Business or Pro. Include what Fieldwork gates or meters (skills or license dispatch, routing, agreements, multi-branch, SMS, inventory) and add the GL and Wisconsin compliance on top.
