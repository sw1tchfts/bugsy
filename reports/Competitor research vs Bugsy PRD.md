# Bugsy's wedge holds; its pricing story breaks

The PRD's central bet survives the October 2026 evidence: across the eight named competitors, Fieldwork, and about ten more pest tools, **no product names Wisconsin, ATCP 29 or the landscape registry, none automates regulatory pesticide notices, and none runs a double-entry ledger with deferred revenue per agreement**. What breaks is the competitive frame drawn around that bet. The competitor set leaves out **Fieldwork**, a pest-first Illinois vendor with two Wisconsin customers visible on public portals and the only software exhibitor at the 2026 Midwest PestCon, and Fieldwork already sells the PRD's "per technician, office users free, published price" model for less: **$442 a month for 8 technicians on its Pro plan, against Bugsy's $632 to $792**. Adding Fieldwork moves the catalog row (CMP-02) from 0 to 1, so "11 of the 13 wedge features" becomes **10 of 13**, and PestPac and FieldRoutes both announced churn analytics in October 2026, which puts the retention row on a clock. The "why now" leans on a ServSuite migration for which no Wisconsin evidence exists and on a list of state reports that is now stale. The market the PRD calls "Unknown" can now be sized: **146 employer firms with $148.4M in 2022 receipts, about 32 to 37 of them in the 3 to 15 technician band, and a Wisconsin-only revenue ceiling near $1M a year even at full share**, so Wisconsin is a proof market rather than the business. The product owner should keep the wedge, the first-customer profile and the winter cutover, and rewrite the competitor set, the positioning counts, the why-now bullets, the price structure and the market-size risk, using the 27 edits in the final section.

## The wedge holds across nine rivals, but its counts need a recount

### Compliance and the ledger remain open everywhere

The PRD's Summary claims that "none of the 8 competitors researched fully supports Wisconsin rules, automatic pesticide notices, restricted-use controls, a built-in general ledger or deferred revenue." That sentence holds for all nine and for the wider market. PestPac's chemical-tracking page names California and Arizona reports plus generic "other state-specific compliance exports", never Wisconsin ([PestPac chemical tracking](https://www.pestpac.com/features/chemical-tracking-software)). FieldRoutes names only California Branch 3 WDO work and the national NPMA-33 form ([FieldRoutes termite page](https://www.fieldroutes.com/solutions/termite-pest-control)). Fieldwork's help center returns no articles for "Wisconsin" ([Fieldwork help search](https://intercom.help/fieldwork/en/?q=Wisconsin)), and Briostack's new comparison pages claim "state-specific WDI/WDO forms (all 50 states)" without mentioning ATCP 29, the registry or posting ([Briostack vs GorillaDesk](https://www.briostack.com/comparison/briostack-vs-gorilladesk/)). A scan of about ten more pest tools found **no product of any kind that names ATCP 29 or the landscape registry**; a search for Wisconsin support returned only the code itself ([Wis. Admin. Code ATCP 29](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/code/admin_code/atcp/020/29/_45?up=1)). The closest thing to a pesticide notice anywhere is a completion email: GorillaDesk triggers send the work order with materials listed ([GorillaDesk triggers](https://intercom.help/gorilladesk/en/articles/2164746-triggers)), and Fieldwork emails the completed work order with optional SDS and label links ([Fieldwork work orders](https://intercom.help/fieldwork/en/articles/1345058-settings-work-orders)). Neither sends a pre-application notice, a 12-hour registry notice, or a notice to residents who are not the account contact.

The accounting half of the wedge is just as intact. A July 2026 scorecard lists FieldRoutes, PestPac, GorillaDesk, Briostack, Fieldwork and Pocomos as having **no general ledger**, and FieldRoutes as lacking "GAAP deferred revenue schedules" ([ERP Scorecard](https://erpscorecard.com/erp-systems/industries/field-services/pest-control)). PestPac calls its own link "an export-based QuickBooks integration, not a live sync" ([PestPac accounting](https://www.pestpac.com/features/accounting-software)), and Fieldwork's sync runs one way into QuickBooks Online ([Fieldwork QuickBooks](https://intercom.help/fieldwork/en/articles/15326708-settings-quickbooks)). Jobber and Housecall Pro answer the gap with people rather than software: Jobber Bookkeeping starts at **$149 a month**, US only ([Jobber Bookkeeping](https://www.getjobber.com/features/bookkeeping/)), and HCP Accounting is run by "bookkeeping experts" with no documented ledger or deferred revenue ([HCP Accounting](https://www.housecallpro.com/features/accounting/)). Cancellation billing is undocumented wherever it was checked: GorillaDesk's Terminate Service voids the linked invoice and deletes the recurring jobs ([GorillaDesk Terminate Service](https://intercom.help/gorilladesk/en/articles/10349402-job-status-terminate-service)), and Jobber tells users to close the job and decide by hand whether to invoice ([Jobber help](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/what-if-a-job-gets-canceled/)).

Nothing on the competitors' 2025 to 2026 roadmaps aims at this gap. WorkWave spent 2026 on its WAIve AI layer, prescriptive analytics, a door-to-door sales engine and churn-risk hubs ([PR Newswire, Oct 20 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/workwave-announces-two-new-ai-driven-analytics-decision-support-for-pestpac-and-realgreen-that-reduce-customer-churn-and-increase-services-per-tech-302913580.html)). FieldRoutes' Ignite 2026 previewed an AI Command Center, churn indicators and same-day dispatch, with no compliance or ledger items ([Ignite 2026 release](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/TTAN/field-routes-to-unveil-next-phase-of-product-innovation-at-ignite-p5x81stcr2wc.html)). GorillaDesk's change log for both years covers AI agents, VoIP, payments and marketing, with no entry on licenses, notices, SDS or accounting ([GorillaDesk change log](https://intercom.help/gorilladesk/en/articles/8420909-gorilladesk-software-change-log-and-updates)), and Fieldwork's 2026 list is an AI receptionist, a phone system, a sales pipeline and new reports ([Fieldwork coming soon](https://intercom.help/fieldwork/en/articles/14741024-coming-soon)). The wedge is open because incumbents invest elsewhere, not because it is hard to build. Fieldwork already produces California's Monthly Summary Use Report and New York's NYSDEC CSV ([Fieldwork NYSDEC](https://intercom.help/fieldwork/en/articles/1454205-preparing-for-your-nysdec-report-submission)), so it is the rival likeliest to add Wisconsin records if Wisconsin proves worth it; nothing on its roadmap says it will.

The edges of the wedge are softer than the PRD's zeros suggest. **Fieldwork fully meets CMP-02**: each material stores the EPA number, active-ingredient percent, label and SDS, and an optional LabelSDS link keeps documents current ([Fieldwork materials](https://intercom.help/fieldwork/en/articles/15400971-settings-materials)); EPA-number capture also now appears in horizontal tools such as ServiceTitan and FieldCamp ([ServiceTitan pest page](https://www.servicetitan.com/industries/pest-control-software); [FieldCamp docs](https://docs.fieldcamp.ai/data-model/chemicals)). Retention analytics (RPT-02) is the row most at risk. On October 20, 2026 PestPac announced a Customer Hub that "scores each account's risk of canceling" and shows callback rate by technician ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/workwave-announces-two-new-ai-driven-analytics-decision-support-for-pestpac-and-realgreen-that-reduce-customer-churn-and-increase-services-per-tech-302913580.html)), although PestPac's own Wavelytics page still labels Decision Intelligence "Coming Soon" ([PestPac Wavelytics](https://www.pestpac.com/wavelytics)); on October 8 FieldRoutes listed "Revenue Retention (upcoming)" with no date ([Ignite 2026 release](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/TTAN/field-routes-to-unveil-next-phase-of-product-innovation-at-ignite-p5x81stcr2wc.html)). The PRD lists RPT-02 as a wedge feature and marks it "Not planned" in the same row, the weakest possible combination. License-based assignment (SCH-04) still counts two full rivals, but skills matching is spreading: GorillaDesk's Mantis matches service types to technician skills in a Growth-plan beta ([GorillaDesk Mantis](https://intercom.help/gorilladesk/en/articles/16069037-mantis-ai-routing-beta)), Fieldwork's Best Fit and Skillsets sit on its Enterprise plan ([Fieldwork billing](https://intercom.help/fieldwork/en/articles/1351253-settings-billing-subscription)), and Smart Service routes by "technician license category" ([Smart Service](https://www.smartservice.com/pest-control-apps)). Bugsy's claim must be **license category and expiry enforced at dispatch, on every plan**, not skill matching. The one Yes on seasonal programs (AGR-02) is weak, since Briostack documents seasonal spacing templates rather than rounds inside a date window ([Briostack scheduling](https://www.briostack.com/pest-control-software/scheduling-routing-software/)), while GorillaDesk sells whole-season mosquito scheduling, season prepay and renewal notices that a Wisconsin mosquito buyer could judge good enough ([GorillaDesk mosquito page](https://gorilladesk.com/industries/mosquito-control-software/)). Briostack also now lists "Technician licensing and product controls" with no detail, the claim most likely to erode CMP-03 and CMP-06 and a question for any demo ([Briostack vs GorillaDesk](https://www.briostack.com/comparison/briostack-vs-gorilladesk/)).

The larger threats to the wedge sit with buyers, not rivals. Accountants' default for deferred revenue is a field service tool plus Sage Intacct, with QuickBooks hitting a "ceiling" on prepaid agreements at volume ([ERP Scorecard](https://erpscorecard.com/erp-systems/industries/field-services/pest-control)); PestPac tells smaller firms its reports "replace the need for QuickBooks entirely" ([PestPac accounting](https://www.pestpac.com/features/accounting-software)); and Smart Service sells pest compliance capture as "a QuickBooks add-on" ([Smart Service](https://www.smartservice.com/pest-control-apps)). On compliance, enforcement is light: DATCP made **96 commercial applicator record inspections in 2025 across 2,412 licensed business locations and opened 2 structural compliance cases**, and 3,576 addresses sat on the landscape registry ([DATCP 2025 annual report](https://datcp.wi.gov/Documents2/2025ACMAnnualReport.pdf)). The PRD's licensed-manager row leads with inspection risk ("a DATCP inspection means pulling paper"); the evidence says the pitch lands better on hours saved and proof of notice.

### Table stakes are universal, and six rivals charge to text

All nine offer a map dispatch board, a mobile app, field payments, autopay and standard reports, so the PRD's "all 8" becomes all 9, with tier caveats: Fieldwork's Google Maps view starts on its $79 Business plan ([Fieldwork billing](https://intercom.help/fieldwork/en/articles/1351253-settings-billing-subscription)), Jobber's reminders and autopay start on Connect ([Jobber pricing](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/)), and Housecall Pro's recurring service plans exist only on MAX ([HCP pricing](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/)). Reminder and on-the-way texts (CX-02, "5" in the PRD) need the most care. FieldRoutes moved up: reminders go by "text messages and phone calls" and its app has had On my way since at least June 2026 ([FieldRoutes pest page](https://www.fieldroutes.com/solutions/pest-control-software); [FieldRoutes App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1583053577)). ServSuite moved down, because its app sends on-my-way emails and text reminders are unconfirmed ([ServSuite App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/servsuite-mobile/id534271165)), and Briostack documents reminders but no on-the-way message ([Briostack scheduling](https://www.briostack.com/pest-control-software/scheduling-routing-software/)). That leaves **7 of 9 offering the feature, and at least 6 of them charging for texting**: PestPac through its Professional tier and notification credits ([PestPac pricing](https://www.pestpac.com/pricing)), Pocomos at $4 a month plus $0.04 a text ([Pocomos SMS](https://pocomos.com/features/sms-communications)), GorillaDesk at $5 a month plus credit packs ([GorillaDesk pricing](https://gorilladesk.com/pricing/)), Fieldwork at $0.03 a segment plus $5 a number ([Fieldwork automated messages](https://intercom.help/fieldwork/en/articles/15209682-settings-automated-messages)), Jobber through Connect, and FieldRoutes through per-message fees that reviewers report ([One House Marketing](https://onehousemarketing.com/fieldroutes-review/)). Briostack bills $0.04 a text and an $11 10DLC fee on top ([Briostack additional pricing](https://www.briostack.com/additionalpricing/)). The baseline files apply the taxonomy's Add-on rule unevenly: `docs/competitors/pestpac.json` scores PestPac's credit-metered texts as Add-on, while `gorilladesk.json` scores GorillaDesk's paid SMS package as Yes. Applied consistently, at most two of nine include reminder texts in the base subscription, which makes **"SMS included" Bugsy's sharpest pricing differentiator**. The crosswalk should report "offered at any price" and "included in the base plan" as separate counts.

The PRD's offline argument ("weak offline modes (Housecall Pro is view-only offline, GorillaDesk caches only the current week)") is accurate for the tools it names and wrong as a category claim. Housecall Pro still says "Editing without cell service or WiFi connection is not supported at this time" ([HCP mobile FAQ](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/4747652-housecall-pro-mobile-faq)). GorillaDesk does store only the current week, but its real limit is that job and invoice details cannot be edited offline, while check-in and check-out times and materials can, which are the core ATCP 29.21 fields ([GorillaDesk offline mode](https://intercom.help/gorilladesk/en/articles/6993151-app-offline-mode)). Pest specialists claim much more: PestPac's app "works at full capacity without cell service" ([PestPac mobile](https://www.pestpac.com/features/pest-control-mobile-app)), and Fieldwork technicians complete work orders offline ([Fieldwork completing a work order](https://intercom.help/fieldwork/en/articles/5318270-fieldwork-office-completing-a-work-order)). Their weakness is reliability, not scope. PestPac's iOS app is rated **2.4 from 150 ratings** ([PestPac App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1211298978)), Fieldwork shipped offline-sync fixes from May through October 2026 ([Fieldwork App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fieldwork-office/id1456789608)), and an October 2026 FieldRoutes reviewer reported reports "wiped by auto-sync" in the same month FieldRoutes shipped offline hardening ([FieldRoutes App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1583053577)). Bugsy should sell its pilot metric (zero lost visits, completion blocked until the legal record is whole) rather than offline as a feature.

Three Phase 1 omissions will surface in every demo. Route optimization comes with GorillaDesk's $49 Basic plan for up to 25 stops ([GorillaDesk pricing](https://gorilladesk.com/pricing/)) and with Pocomos for up to 10 trucks ([Pocomos route optimization](https://pocomos.com/features/route-optimization)), so the PRD's view that "manual dispatch with simple sequencing (Phase 1) works at this size" should appear in the positioning table as a named gap with a sales answer. Six of nine ship a customer portal at Yes, including Fieldwork from its Startup plan ([Fieldwork pricing](https://fieldworkhq.com/pricing/)), against Bugsy's Phase 2. On AI, Jobber launched Claude and ChatGPT connectors for all customers on September 25, 2026 ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/jobber-launches-mcp-bringing-home-and-commercial-service-data-and-workflows-directly-into-chatgpt-and-claude-302890385.html)), GorillaDesk released an MCP server in August 2026 ([GorillaDesk change log](https://intercom.help/gorilladesk/en/articles/8420909-gorilladesk-software-change-log-and-updates)), Fieldwork followed in September ([Fieldwork MCP](https://intercom.help/fieldwork/en/articles/16795867-using-the-fieldwork-mcp-connector)), and AI receptionists sold to pest firms advertise FieldRoutes, PestPac and GorillaDesk connections ([JustCall guide](https://justcall.io/blog/best-ai-receptionists-pest-control.html)). Keeping "AI features" out of Phase 1 stays defensible, but a public API or webhooks belongs on the Phase 2 list so those tools can attach.

## Why now rests on Wisconsin's gap, not ServSuite's sunset

The first "why now" bullet opens with "No competitor researched shows Wisconsin support", which holds across all nine and the wider scan. Its second sentence, "The only state reports named are California, Arizona and New York (PestPac) and California WDO (FieldRoutes)", is stale. Fieldwork produces California's Monthly Summary Use Report and New York's NYSDEC annual CSV ([Fieldwork California report](https://intercom.help/fieldwork/en/articles/1086359-california-monthly-summary-use-report-codes-and-rules); [Fieldwork NYSDEC](https://intercom.help/fieldwork/en/articles/1454205-preparing-for-your-nysdec-report-submission)), Briostack claims "state-specific WDI/WDO forms (all 50 states)" and a direct California Department of Agriculture integration ([Briostack vs GorillaDesk](https://www.briostack.com/comparison/briostack-vs-gorilladesk/)), and Pocomos' new Custom Documents name Arizona, Texas, Georgia, Virginia and Connecticut forms while warning that they do "not replace state-approved forms" ([Pocomos Custom Documents](https://help.pocomos.com/en/articles/17771127-custom-documents-state-forms-inspections-diagrams-and-signatures)). The accurate version is stronger: state formats are spreading, yet **none covers ATCP 29 records, registry notices or posting**.

The second bullet ("FieldRoutes is sunsetting ServSuite with no public end date, so its users must migrate anyway. A forced migration is a buying moment") is half right. FieldRoutes does say "ServSuite by FieldRoutes is sunsetting" with no end date ([FieldRoutes migration page](https://fieldroutes.com/lp/ss-migration/free-demo)), but nothing forces anyone yet: a fourth migration cohort is being offered an "updated incentive" ([FieldRoutes cohort 4](https://fieldroutes.com/lp/ss-migration/cohort-4/free-demo)), the ServSuite product page still promises "no change to your software or service" ([ServSuite page](https://www.fieldroutes.com/servsuite-by-fieldroutes)), and the ServSuite app shipped a fix release days before October 29, 2026 ([ServSuite App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/servsuite-mobile/id534271165)). More than 400 companies had already moved by 2023, among them **Kwik Kill Pest Control of Madison** (15 technicians), which called the switch to FieldRoutes "a cakewalk" ([FieldRoutes Kwik Kill case study](https://www.fieldroutes.com/resources/case-studies/kwik-kill-pest-control-case-study); [CaseStudies.com](https://www.casestudies.com/company/fieldroutes-servicetitan/case-study/kwik-kill-pest-control-customer-case-study)). A crawl of 38 Wisconsin independents' websites drawn from city directories found no ServSuite portal ([Superpages Madison listing](https://www.superpages.com/madison-wi/pest-control-services)), and Pocomos now runs a page aimed at FieldRoutes users with month-to-month terms ([Pocomos vs FieldRoutes](https://pocomos.com/vs-fieldroutes)). In Wisconsin the ServSuite moment is a footnote, not a reason.

The third bullet lists category complaints. Add-on fees hold up best: Briostack publishes a schedule that includes $150 per custom document and $500 for a database backup ([Briostack additional pricing](https://www.briostack.com/additionalpricing/)), and a February 2026 reviewer wrote that features "presented as included during signup later appeared as additional charges" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/briostack.com)). Bugs after updates are documented for FieldRoutes, whose September 2026 update removed the daily production value ([FieldRoutes App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1583053577)). "Opaque pricing" is now true only of PestPac, FieldRoutes, Briostack and ServSuite; **5 of 9 publish prices**, and Pocomos has moved to published, month-to-month plans ([Pocomos FAQ](https://pocomos.com/faq)). The sentence "PestPac users report QuickBooks reconciliation gaps" could not be verified in any 2025 or 2026 review; what is verifiable is PestPac's own description of an export-based, non-live QuickBooks link ([PestPac accounting](https://www.pestpac.com/features/accounting-software)), and the PRD should say that instead.

The fourth bullet ("Horizontal tools (Jobber, Housecall Pro) are cheap and polished but have no device programs, labels, safety data sheets or state reports") is right on polish (Jobber shows **4.8 from 13,861 App Store reviews** ([Jobber pest page](https://www.getjobber.com/industries/pest-control-software/))) and on labels, SDS and state reports, but "cheap" ignores tier gating. Reminders and autopay start on Jobber Connect at $99 a month on annual billing or $139 monthly for one user ([Jobber pricing](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/)), and recurring service plans exist only on Housecall Pro MAX at $299 annual or $329 monthly ([HCP pricing](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/)). Jobber does have a chemical log with registration numbers, but its help article says "There is no chemical tracking in the mobile app" ([Jobber chemical tracking](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/chemical-tracking/)), and Housecall Pro offers a printable paper log template instead ([HCP log template](https://www.housecallpro.com/pest-control/templates-calculators/log-book-template)). Housecall Pro's first trade packages, launched July 2026, cover HVAC, plumbing and electrical, not pest ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/15/3327769/0/en/housecall-pro-launches-trade-specific-software-packages-for-hvac-plumbing-and-electrical-businesses.html)).

Better why-now material exists. Every rival's 2025 to 2026 releases went to AI, analytics, payments and sales rather than compliance or accounting (sources in the first section), which is a timing argument in its own right. Residential economics are tightening: Rollins reported only 3.6% residential organic growth in Q2 2026 and said "lead volume declined in the quarter" ([Rollins Q2 2026](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/3411683/rollins-inc-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results)), which raises the value of the retention, re-service and contract-position reporting the PRD plans. Wisconsin's market is also growing, with pest control employment up 24% from 2023 to 2025 ([BLS QCEW](https://data.bls.gov/cew/data/api/2025/1/industry/561710.csv)).

## About 35 Wisconsin firms fit the profile, and rivals already serve them

The PRD's "Unknown: the size of the Wisconsin market" can be replaced with Census figures. Wisconsin had **146 employer pest control firms with $148.4M in receipts in 2022**, and six national firms took 48% of those receipts ([Census SUSB 2022](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/tables/2022/us_state_6digitnaics_2022.txt)). Another 269 one-person businesses earned $16.0M in 2024 ([Census Nonemployer Statistics 2024](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/nonemployer-statistics/datasets/2024/historical-datasets/nonemp24st.zip)), and the industry averaged about 1,550 employees in 2025 ([BLS QCEW](https://data.bls.gov/cew/data/api/2025/1/industry/561710.csv)). The PRD's proposed method, a DATCP count of businesses with 7.1 applicators, cannot be run from published data: DATCP reports **2,412 pesticide business locations across every category** (agriculture, turf, right-of-way and structural together), about five times the 453 pest establishments Census counts ([DATCP 2025 annual report](https://datcp.wi.gov/Documents2/2025ACMAnnualReport.pdf)), and the MyDATCP applicator export link was empty on October 29, 2026 ([MyDATCP search](https://mydatcp.wisconsin.gov/Search)). A direct request to DATCP for 7.1 and 7.3 counts is still worth sending, but a raw DATCP total would overstate the market about fivefold.

The first-customer profile ("independent, residential-first Wisconsin pest companies with one branch and 3 to 15 technicians") is supported but small. Firms with 5 to 9 employees (19 firms, about $873K in receipts each) and 10 to 19 employees (13 firms, about $1.79M each), plus part of the five firms with 20 to 99 employees, give **about 32 to 37 firms**, and firm and establishment counts match in those classes, so they are single-location ([Census SUSB 2022](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/tables/2022/us_state_6digitnaics_2022.txt)). The PRD's "first 10 tenants" would be 27 to 31% of that pool. Excluding 1 and 2 technician owner-operators is well supported, since 102 of the 146 employer firms have fewer than 5 employees. Residential-first cannot be tested from public data.

The ceiling follows. Applying the national share of technicians among pest employees to Wisconsin gives roughly 940 technicians in 2025, an estimate rather than a published figure; at $79 to $99 a month that is **about $0.89M to $1.12M a year at 100% share including national brands**, about $0.54M to $0.68M without them, and roughly $175K to $330K within the 3 to 15 technician band (arithmetic on the cited Census and BLS data). The four neighbors (Minnesota, Illinois, Michigan, Iowa) hold 770 employer firms and $1.11B in receipts, about 7.5 times Wisconsin, with Illinois alone at 330 firms and $527.1M ([Census SUSB 2022](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/tables/2022/us_state_6digitnaics_2022.txt)). The pool is also shrinking: in 2025 Greenix bought Spidexx's Milwaukee office and Rove's Milwaukee and Madison operations ([Riata Capital: Spidexx](https://www.riatacapital.com/news/greenix-pest-control-expands-wisconsin-presence-with-acquisition-of-spidexx-pest-control); [Riata Capital: Rove](https://www.riatacapital.com/news/greenix-pest-control-expands-presence-with-acquisition-of-rove-pest-control)), Plunkett's bought Milwaukee's Horizon ([PMP](https://www.mypmp.net/plunketts-acquires-horizon-pest-management/)), and Ecolab bought Guardian ([PCT](https://www.pctonline.com/news/ecolab-acquires-guardian-pest-solutions)), and roll-up entrants run PestPac portals ([Greenix](https://greenixpc.com/)). The risk row "The Wisconsin market is small ... keep a second state as configuration" understates this; the second state needs choosing and sizing now.

The PRD says target firms "run on spreadsheets, a horizontal tool or ServSuite." Of 38 sampled independents, 30 expose no software at all, which fits spreadsheets or paper but is only a lower bound. Where software is visible, it is **GorillaDesk at three firms** (Bourn Pest Solutions, whose owner sits on the WPCA board, plus Nexus and 1st Choice Pest Control of Appleton) ([Bourn Pest Solutions](https://bournpestsolutions.com/); [Nexus Pest Solutions](https://nexuspestsolutions.com/)), **Fieldwork at two** (1st Choice Pest Solutions of Eau Claire and On Target of La Crosse) ([1st Choice portal](https://pestsolutions1stchoice.serviceworkportal.com/sessions/new); [On Target portal](https://ontargetpestcontrol.serviceworkportal.com/sessions/new)), and PestPac, Briostack and Service Autopilot at one each ([Fenix Pest Control](https://fenixpestcontrol.com/); [Werner Pest](https://wernerpest.com/); [Pest Badger](https://pestbadger.com/)), plus FieldRoutes at Kwik Kill. No Jobber, Housecall Pro or ServSuite use was detected. GorillaDesk is not only the 1 to 2 technician tool the "Not first" bullet implies: it showcases Midwest pest firms with 13 to 27 technicians ([GorillaDesk pest page](https://gorilladesk.com/industries/pest-control-software/)) and lists Mosquito Police of Antigo, Wisconsin, with 8 ([GorillaDesk mosquito page](https://gorilladesk.com/industries/mosquito-control-software/)). At 8 technicians Bugsy costs **1.4 to 1.8 times Fieldwork Pro** and more than every published option except Fieldwork Enterprise; it sits near "per-user pest software" only against PestPac and possibly FieldRoutes, the products the target segment is least likely to run. At 3 technicians the gap widens in relative terms: Bugsy's $237 to $297 compares with $197 for Fieldwork Pro. The absolute cost is modest, about 0.45 to 0.55% of revenue for an 8-technician firm earning roughly $1.7M at Wisconsin's $127K to $133K of receipts per employee ([Census SUSB 2022](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/tables/2022/us_state_6digitnaics_2022.txt)), so the problem is relative price, not affordability. The pricing model itself is not new: Fieldwork already charges only for "users who are out in the field" and makes office staff free ([Fieldwork pricing](https://fieldworkhq.com/pricing/)), and GorillaDesk, Fieldwork, Pocomos, Jobber and Housecall Pro all publish prices. What still differentiates is **"all-inclusive"**: Fieldwork gates service agreements to Pro, routing to Business or Pro (its own pages conflict), and skills matching, inventory and online booking to Enterprise, and it meters texting ([Fieldwork billing](https://intercom.help/fieldwork/en/articles/1351253-settings-billing-subscription)). Bugsy should position against Fieldwork Enterprise and GorillaDesk Growth, not against their entry tiers.

The price unit needs as much attention as the price. Wisconsin pest employment peaks at **1.33 to 1.44 times its winter trough**, against about 1.1 nationally ([BLS QCEW](https://data.bls.gov/cew/data/api/2025/2/industry/561710.csv)), so billing "per active technician" recreates the seasonal swing the PRD criticizes in Pocomos, where on-hold customers still count and a reviewer complained that customer-count pricing "made budgeting harder" ([Pocomos pricing](https://pocomos.com/pricing); [Capterra Pocomos](https://www.capterra.com/p/133292/Pocomos/)). It also charges a two-person crew twice, whereas GorillaDesk bills crews that travel together only for the crew leader ([GorillaDesk plans](https://intercom.help/gorilladesk/en/articles/431660-plans-pricing)); the PRD's own `visit_technicians` change will make trainee-plus-applicator crews common. A base-plus-per-technician price, with technicians counted on a trailing 12-month peak, helpers and trainees on a crew free, and no 2-technician minimum, fixes all three problems. As an illustration to test rather than a research finding, $149 plus $69 for each technician after the first gives $287 at 3, $632 at 8 and $1,115 at 15, undercutting Fieldwork Enterprise at every size while keeping the 8-technician price at the hypothesis floor.

The PRD's pricing table needs five corrections and one new row. Pocomos now publishes **$109 (0 to 20 active customers) to $275 (301 to 500), then $0.16 per customer over 500**, month to month, replacing the PRD's "$59 (up to 20 customers) to $225 (300 to 500)" ([Pocomos pricing](https://pocomos.com/pricing)). FieldRoutes' "from about $350 a month" is, per G2, $350 per 1,000 active customers, and the vendor publishes only "Request Pricing" ([FieldRoutes pricing](https://fieldroutes.com/pricing)). Briostack's "about $95 per office user and $45 per technician" cannot be verified because its source now returns 404 ([LawnStarter](https://lawnstarter.com/blog/?p=52157)); Briostack now sells "concurrent licensing by active users" ([Briostack vs GorillaDesk](https://www.briostack.com/comparison/briostack-vs-gorilladesk/)) under a one-year auto-renewing term with an early-termination fee equal to the monthly fees for the remaining months ([Briostack terms](https://www.briostack.com/terms/)). Fieldwork belongs in the table as "base plus per technician, $39 + $19 (Solo) to $149 + $79 (Enterprise), office free, month to month." ## Conclusion

The research shifts where Bugsy's defensible ground lies. The durable core is narrower than the 13-row wedge: it is the pairing of Wisconsin-complete records and notices with per-agreement contract accounting, the two things no competitor documents and no 2025 to 2026 roadmap targets. Catalog fields, skills-based dispatch and churn dashboards are already being built by others, so they belong in the pitch as parity items done better, not as the reason to buy. That also changes whom Bugsy sells against. The first ten deals will be argued against Fieldwork and GorillaDesk, low-cost tools that customers like, so the sales case is not "modern software instead of a dying ServSuite" but "we cost more than Fieldwork Pro because we replace the paper notice log and the accountant's deferred-revenue spreadsheet." Whether Wisconsin owners will pay that premium is the one question no public source answers, and the pilot exit interview and the 3 to 5 discovery calls should test it explicitly with Fieldwork and GorillaDesk users in the room.

The market numbers carry a strategic implication the PRD has not yet drawn. A Wisconsin-only product tops out near $1M a year, and the target band is about three dozen firms that roll-ups are actively buying, so the second state is a Phase 2 product decision rather than a later configuration exercise. Illinois, with 330 firms and $527.1M in receipts and home to Fieldwork itself, is the largest nearby independent pool, while Minnesota's establishments average twice Wisconsin's headcount but national firms hold 62% of its receipts ([Census SUSB 2022](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/tables/2022/us_state_6digitnaics_2022.txt); [Census CBP 2023](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/datasets/2023/cbp23st.zip)). Picking the state now would let the rules-as-data design, the price unit and the PestCon pitch all be set once, for the market Bugsy will actually need.
