# Pest control domain

## The business in one paragraph

A pest control company is a route-based subscription business. Most revenue comes from recurring service agreements (the largest public operator reports about 75% recurring). The first visit costs more because it includes an inspection and a heavy first treatment; recurring visits are usually quarterly. Plans include free re-services if pests come back between visits. Profit depends on route density (12 to 18 stops a day within 10 to 15 miles is a common benchmark) and retention (80%+ a year is strong; under 2% monthly attrition is top quartile).

## Service lines

| Line | How it is sold | What makes it different |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General pest | Recurring plan | Agreements, routes, re-services |
| Rodent | Recurring plan or one-time; commercial station programs | Device tracking |
| Termite | Inspection, per-job treatment, then a yearly warranty or monitoring | Warranties, bait station checks, property diagrams; mostly southern Wisconsin |
| Mosquito and tick | Seasonal recurring, spring to fall | Season windows, landscape notice rules, tax question |
| Bed bug | Per-job bid, several visits | Customer prep checklists |
| Wildlife | Per-job trapping, exclusion and repairs | Landowner authorization, bat blackout, materials |

## Scope

Out of scope: lawn care, tree and shrub care, and other trades. Mosquito and tick yard spraying, termite and wildlife are in scope because pest companies sell them. Competitors that also serve lawn care or other trades are compared on their pest features only.

## Glossary

- **PCO / PMP**: pest control operator, pest management professional; the company or its technicians.
- **Initial**: the first visit on a plan; longer and priced higher.
- **Recurring visit**: a scheduled visit generated by the agreement.
- **Re-service / callback**: a free return visit between scheduled visits when pests come back.
- **Agreement / plan / contract**: the customer's recurring service commitment.
- **Route**: one technician's ordered stops for a day. **Route density**: stops per day per area.
- **Due window**: the date range a recurring visit should fall in (for example "any day in April").
- **Attrition / churn**: cancelled agreements as a share of active ones.
- **Production pay**: technician pay as a percentage of the revenue they service, usually with a draw; hours must still be recorded.
- **D2D / summer sales**: door-to-door sales teams selling plans, common in residential growth.
- **IPM**: integrated pest management; inspection and prevention first, targeted treatment second.
- **EPA registration number**: the federal ID on every pesticide label; required on records.
- **Label**: the legally binding product instructions. **SDS**: safety data sheet.
- **RUP**: restricted-use pesticide; only certified applicators may use it.
- **REI**: restricted entry interval; time before people may re-enter a treated area.
- **Perimeter / barrier treatment**: exterior band application around a structure.
- **Bait station / rodent station / ILT (insect light trap)**: devices serviced on commercial accounts, usually barcoded and mapped.
- **Device map**: floor plan showing every device; food safety auditors compare it to the floor.
- **AIB, SQF, BRC**: food safety audit schemes commercial customers face.
- **WDI / WDO report**: wood-destroying insect (organism) inspection report, often for real estate sales.
- **Exclusion**: sealing entry points; for bats, one-way doors.
- **Rounds**: the numbered applications in a mosquito or tick season program.

## Seasonality in Wisconsin

Outdoor work (mosquito, tick, perimeter) stops in winter. Companies keep revenue and technician pay steady with interior-only winter visits, skipped months, and flat monthly billing. Spring brings sign-ups and upsells (mosquito and tick).

## Users

Owner or admin, office staff, dispatcher or service manager, technician, sales rep, licensed manager (compliance), bookkeeper or outside accountant; customer-side residential customers, commercial customer users and property managers.
